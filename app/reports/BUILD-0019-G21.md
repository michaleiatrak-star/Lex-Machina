## Goal

Stop treating every amendment announced after a consolidated text as automatically effective for every legal-state date.

G21 distinguishes:

- `FUTURE` — amendment is not yet effective on the target legal-state date;
- `EFFECTIVE` — amendment is already effective;
- `UNKNOWN` — official metadata is insufficient to establish effect date.

## Rule

A future amendment must not invalidate an earlier legal state merely because it has already been promulgated.

At the same time Lex Machina must not synthesize amended statutory wording from model memory.

Therefore:

- FUTURE → may continue using the selected official text;
- EFFECTIVE → BLOCKED until deterministic amendment overlay is implemented;
- UNKNOWN → BLOCKED fail-closed.

## Official temporal signals

The runtime checks official amendment metadata and keeps separate concepts separate:

- promulgation / announcement;
- ELI relation date;
- `entryIntoForce`;
- `validFrom`.

Promulgation alone is not treated as entry into force.

Where multiple official effect signals exist, the resolver takes the earliest signal conservatively so it cannot silently classify an already-effective amendment as future.

## Historical-state interaction

The same classification applies to explicit `asOf` queries.

A text repealed today may still be used for a past legal state only when:
- the act/text was then in force; and
- no EFFECTIVE/UNKNOWN post-text amendment invalidates that selected historical state.

This preserves the user-required historical exception without weakening current-law safety.

## Limitation retained intentionally

G21 does **not** compose amended statutory text.

An EFFECTIVE amendment remains blocking until a later deterministic overlay engine can establish the operative wording from official amendment material.
